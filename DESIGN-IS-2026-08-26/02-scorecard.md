# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Overtone-aware sustained locking improves a familiar tuner pattern (`01-evidence.md`, Innovative).
   Justification: It refreshes an established interaction with a clear practice benefit but does not establish a new category pattern.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: The default task needs one exercise choice and one Start action before automatic reference/sing/repeat behavior (`01-evidence.md`, Useful).
   Justification: The primary practice task is direct, distance-readable, and contains no decoy action in its main path.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: A coherent token system is weakened by low-contrast microtype, duplicated target treatment, and stacked glass/glow decoration (`01-evidence.md`, Aesthetic).
   Justification: At least three visible inconsistencies prevent the surface from scoring as a merely minor polish issue.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Multiple specialist labels and a silent marker displayed at perfect center create ambiguity (`01-evidence.md`, Understandable).
   Justification: More than one control or state requires prior musical/UI knowledge to interpret correctly.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Practice chrome is quiet and reduced, but layered glass, glows, chips, and persistent shell status remain visible (`01-evidence.md`, Unobtrusive).
   Justification: The target stays dominant, although the chrome does not fully disappear into the background.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Skip is recorded as a failed attempt, and “continue when ready” advances after 750ms (`01-evidence.md`, Honest).
   Justification: Forced continuity plus multiple label-to-behavior mismatches meet the rubric's score-1 threshold.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Shared tokens and stable music language coexist with a strong glass/blur/glow trend marker (`01-evidence.md`, Long-lasting).
   Justification: One prominent contemporary styling marker keeps it from the timeless score.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: All major state classes exist, but feedback is unreadable at its normal duration and some ARIA labels lack roles (`01-evidence.md`, Thorough).
   Justification: The state model is broad, with one materially rough state rather than several absent states.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The renderer is 1.286MB raw JS and uses many concurrent backdrop-filter surfaces (`01-evidence.md`, Environmentally friendly).
   Justification: The bundle falls in the rubric's 500KB–2MB band despite no external traffic or idle animation.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 1/3
    Evidence: Target, phase, setup summary, and tuner meaning are each expressed through several removable duplicates (`01-evidence.md`, As little design as possible).
    Justification: Four or more elements can be removed without breaking the primary practice task.

## Total

16/30
