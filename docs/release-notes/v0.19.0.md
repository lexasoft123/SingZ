Nothing in this release touches a song you already have. No grid re-derives, no
line re-tracks, no lyrics move. Everything below is new surface.

Everywhere — SingZ grew a second half:

- **Vocal training.** There is a switch at the top of the window now — Songs,
  and Vocal training — and a two-tab bar at the bottom of the phone. The second
  one is a practice room: it plays you a note, listens to you sing it, and
  tells you what you actually did.

  Pick what you want to hear more clearly. On the desktop that is six things:
  match a note, notes in a key, intervals, chord tones, arpeggios, or a mixed
  rehearsal that rotates through them. The phone has four of them — single
  notes, intervals, notes in a chord, and carrying the line through an
  arpeggio. Then pick a key, and tell it your comfortable range — today's easy
  working notes, it asks for, not your maximum.

  Then it asks, one of three ways. **Imitate**: it plays the note, you sing it
  back. **Find the note yourself**: it tells you what to sing — degree 5, a
  rising sixth, the third of that chord — and plays the key, or the note you
  start from, or the chord it belongs to. Never the answer. **Listen and choose**:
  no singing at all, just say what you heard. The phone offers two of them,
  under its own names — Imitate, and Identify for the listening one. On the desktop, Listen and choose is also what you are
  left with if there is no microphone to be found — the singing modes grey out
  rather than handing you a practice room you cannot use.

  It is not easily impressed. You have to hold your voice inside ten cents for
  a second and a half before it moves on — and if ten cents is not the argument
  you want to be having today, the tolerance goes to ±20, or down to ±5. It
  ignores the first 150 ms while you find the note, so scooping in is allowed;
  what it grades is where you settled. Land somewhere else and it says which
  somewhere: a different tone of the same chord, a note outside the chord
  entirely, or simply not steady enough to call. Singing it in your own octave
  is fine — both the desktop and the phone fold octaves before judging you, so
  a bass answering a prompt written high is answering it.

- **It knows what song you were just singing.** With a song loaded, training
  opens with a card for it: prepare for this one, in its key, transposition
  included — its notes, its intervals, its chords, or a mixed warm-up. Confirm
  the key first if SingZ never worked one out.

- **Your practice history is yours and stays on the machine.** Completed
  sessions only: how many, how many notes landed, and whether you tend sharp,
  flat, or neither — plus a suggestion of what to work on next. It lives beside
  the app's own settings rather than in your library, so it is not synced: the
  computer and the phone each keep their own. Your microphone is analysed live
  and never recorded, here or anywhere else in the app.

Mac and Windows — the microphone SingZ can actually hear:

- **Training listens through the same C++ core that reads your stems** —
  WASAPI on Windows at the smallest period the driver will give it, CoreAudio
  on the Mac, one lane of it at the rate the hardware runs. Your voice never
  leaves that core: what comes back out of it is pitch, confidence and a
  level, and nothing else. The Settings meter below is the same capture; the
  sing-along pitch strip in a song is still the browser's, which is why it
  keeps the echo cancellation it has always had.

  If you already had a microphone chosen in 0.18.0, open Settings → Audio once
  after updating. What was saved is a browser device id, which means nothing to
  the core, and matching it to the real interface is something Settings does
  while it is open — until it has, training uses the browser's capture as
  before.

- **Pick the channel, not just the box.** If your microphone is on input 5 of a
  sixteen-channel interface, Settings → Audio now has a channel picker in the
  row under the device — on a mono input it simply says so — and it means the
  lane you chose. Before this, everything SingZ listened to was whatever
  arrived on channel 1 of the device, which on an interface with more than a
  pair of inputs is very often not the one your microphone is plugged into.
  The choice carries into the sing-along pitch strip as well, not only into
  training.

- **A level meter, so you can see the microphone is alive** before you find out
  the hard way. It opens with Settings, ticks at −48, −24, −12 and 0, holds
  your peaks for a beat and then lets them fall, and bottoms out at −72 dBFS.
  Choosing a device or a channel moves it immediately — that is the whole
  point of it.

- **And when it can't open, it says which "can't".** Blocked in privacy
  settings, unplugged mid-preview, saved on a machine where that device isn't
  plugged in, or asked for a lane that interface hasn't got — four different
  sentences, because "the meter isn't moving" is not a diagnosis. The last two
  carry on previewing the system default, and whatever lane that device does
  have, so there is something alive on screen while you work out where your
  interface went.

iPhone and Android — the phone behaves like a phone:

- **Back is the swipe your phone already has.** The navigation is the
  platform's own stack now rather than a hand-rolled one, so the edge swipe
  works, feels right, and — the part that matters on a phone holding six
  decoded stems — a swipe you change your mind about halfway leaves the song
  loaded and playing, while one you finish frees it.

- **Add a song is a real sheet now**, sized to what is in it, capped to the
  screen when there is more of it than that, and moved by the keyboard rather
  than measured against it. The Log is a native presentation now too, where it
  used to be a panel the app drew itself. 0.18.0 got the keyboard out of
  the way of that sheet by measuring it on both platforms and lifting the panel
  by hand; this hands the whole question to the OS, which was always the one
  holding the answer.

- **Training listens the way each phone listens best.** On Android that is
  AAudio through the same C++ core the desktop uses — one lane, at the device's
  own rate, and the microphone bytes never cross into the app. On iPhone it is
  one mono lane at 16 kHz, a great deal more than pitch needs, and the
  interesting part is underneath: the audio session belongs to playback, so
  SingZ borrows it, sets it up for recording, and hands it back when you leave,
  verifying the handover in both directions rather than hoping. Nothing is
  recorded on either.
