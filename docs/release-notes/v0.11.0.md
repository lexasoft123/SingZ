v0.11.0 — the beat gets a second opinion

The metronome got a colleague, and sync went on a diet.

## The count-in grew a second brain

The homegrown beat ear now has a colleague: both splitter packs ship a small neural beat model (Beat This!, MIT — thank you, CPJKU), and SingZ fuses the two. Our detector still runs the show where it's confident; the model repairs the grid where the homegrown cues go quiet — organ intros, drumless verses, waltzes. Measured on the whole test library before shipping: songs the old detector already got right stayed byte-identical, and the chronic drifters stopped drifting.

Also in the counting department:

- Chord changes get a vote on where "1" is, and a band that slips phase mid-song finally gets re-phased instead of staying politely wrong to the end.
- Beat analysis is pinned to 44.1 kHz, so your grid no longer depends on which audio device happened to be plugged in that day.
- Downbeat cues listen to both stereo channels now (one song was voting entirely on the left one).
- The metronome's accent on "1" can be turned off, for when it gets bossy.
- Analysis shows one progress bar for the whole job — melody, then beats — instead of finishing and then thinking some more.

**One-time pack update:** the beat model rides inside the splitter pack, so the app will ask you to download the new pack once — 246–274 MB depending on platform, roughly 70 MB more than before (that's the model).

## Sync stopped doing cardio

- A library that hasn't changed syncs in **3 requests** — whether you have 5 songs or 500. Stem fingerprints live in the project file now, so a clean sync reads zero audio bytes; iCloud evicting your stems no longer triggers a full re-upload panic.
- The desktop reconciles Drive on every launch, so a sync that died mid-run heals itself next start.
- Phones list the whole library in 3 requests too (was 3 *per song*), open it instantly from the stored catalog, and a refresh that dies mid-listing keeps the old catalog instead of wiping it. No more surprise "loading from Google Drive" on a cold start in the parking garage.

## Pick where the sound comes out

There's a settings window now: choose your playback device and your microphone. Works on Windows too, where device selection has… a personality. End-to-end tested on both platforms so it stays working.

## Small joys

- The pitch view resizes — drag its top edge and everything drawn scales with it.
- Rename the tracks you added. The label changes, not a byte moves: Drive doesn't re-upload, phones don't re-download.
- The melody line stopped folding correct notes into the wrong octave, and silence no longer pretends to be pitch.
- Lane waveforms scan every sample now — identical drum hits finally draw identical heights.
- If the lyrics service has a bad day, "no lyrics found" is no longer carved in stone — the app quietly asks again later. And the AI lyrics aligner installs properly on Windows.
- The loading screen survives files named `Artist_-_Track_(Official_Video)_320kbps_79576145.wav` without the layout falling over.
