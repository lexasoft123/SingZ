v0.10.0 — bring your own lanes

Sing it in six, count it in seconds, and bring your own lanes.

## The metronome finally knows where "1" is

Beat detection grew ears. Instead of trusting the kick drum alone (which, it turns out, lies about half the time), it now listens to everything that knows the answer — where the band crashes in, where the bass changes chords, where your lyrics land — and lets them vote. The practical upshots:

- Accents land on the actual downbeat now. Soldier Of Fortune and Wanted Dead Or Alive were confidently wrong by half a bar; not anymore.
- 6/8 is a real meter here. Nothing Else Matters gets counted in six the way it's felt, not hammered into 4/4 with the accent on that big tom.
- Songs that genuinely shift their bars after a long pause (Turn The Page's last chorus comes back two beats "off" — on purpose) stay right on both sides of the pause.

Songs you've already saved quietly re-check themselves next time you open them (about half a second, you won't notice). Anything you tapped or set by hand is treated as gospel and left alone.

## Count-ins for songs that refuse to have a beat

Some songs are rubato — The Music Of The Night has no steady pulse, never did, and SingZ won't insult it by inventing one. But you still need to know when to breathe. So: songs without a beat track now offer a **3 s** or **6 s** count-in — ticks once per second, and the music enters exactly one second after the last tick. Slowing the song down for practice doesn't stretch your countdown; a second is a second.

## Your cues, at the speed of sound

- **The count-in dots follow your ears now.** On Bluetooth, the dots used to flip ahead of what you heard — the click was still in the air somewhere over the coffee table. Each dot now fills exactly when its click reaches you, and the dots hold until the music itself is audible.
- **Long instrumental gap? Ticking countdown.** Any wait over 5 seconds counts down in seconds ("14 s… 13 s…") and hands over to the familiar dots at 3. No more staring at a guitar solo wondering whether you missed your entrance.

## Precise alignment stopped hallucinating in outros

On big stacked-choir endings, Precise could shove the last lines into the silence after the music died (Wanted Dead Or Alive's finale allegedly happened five seconds after the band left). Words that land in vocal silence are now ignored as evidence, and those lines keep their verified timing instead. If a song of yours ended weirdly after the four-minute mark, hit **Precise** once more — it'll make amends.

## Bring your own lanes

Add your own audio files to a project — a backing choir, a click from your producer, last week's take you want to sing against — and they show up as extra lanes: synced, mixable, mutable, loopable, just like the six stems. The phone plays them too: they sync through Drive and stream like everything else.

## Phone, standing on its own two bars

- The library opens instantly with no signal — the catalog is remembered, not re-fetched.
- A song downloads **once**. Downloaded stems now live where the OS won't quietly evict them under storage pressure, and re-downloads happen only when the file actually changed (checked by hash, not by wishful thinking).
- Everything above — the seconds count-in, the countdown, the honest dots — works on the phone, latency-corrected for whatever's in your ears.
