Android:

- **The download is half the size.** The APK was 234 MB. Of that, 121 MB —
  more than the entire rest of the app — was the audio engine, ONNX Runtime,
  Hermes and the rest built a second and third time for x86 and x86-64: the
  chips emulators run on, and no phone does. SingZ has been shipping them to
  your phone since the first Android build, where they sat unread. They are
  gone. The new APK should land near 113 MB.

  If you install from Google Play this changes nothing about your download —
  Play has always sent each phone only its own slice, and yours was never the
  x86 one. It is the sideloaded APK from the Releases page that halves, which
  is most of you.

- **Intel Chromebooks pay for this one.** A Chromebook running Android apps on
  an Intel chip no longer finds a native build, so ChromeOS translates the ARM
  one instead. SingZ stays installable there — Google's own advice is that
  shipping both ARM builds, which we do, keeps a Chromebook covered — but
  translated code is slower and harder on the battery, and this app is mostly
  C++ doing arithmetic: splitting a song on an Intel Chromebook will take
  noticeably longer than it used to. ARM Chromebooks are unaffected and never
  needed the x86 build at all.

Nothing in the app itself changed in this release. Not one line of what you
see, hear or tap is different from 0.16.1 — this is the same app with two
copies of its engine taken out of the box it ships in. Behind that, the build
that produces it also stopped spending a third of its time compiling those
copies, and a compiler cache that had never once worked was fixed, which is
nobody's problem but ours and is the real reason this version exists.

iPhone, Mac and Windows: nothing at all for you here. Your computer will still
offer 0.16.2 — the Mac asks, Windows takes it on the next restart — and either
is fine; it is the same app you already have.
