Windows:
- Songs now split on your GeForce RTX (30xx or newer). A four-minute song
  takes about six seconds — not a typo, we timed it twice on two machines.
  The first song teaches the engine your exact card (about half a minute,
  once per machine); every song after that is just fast.
- No RTX? Splits run on the processor exactly as before — a couple of
  minutes per song — and the leaner new model gets going quicker there too.
- The splitter pack went on a diet: 296 MB instead of 525 for the splitter,
  the beat detector and the whole GPU engine. One-time re-download, then it
  owes you nothing.
- DirectML is gone. We gave it every chance on every machine this family
  owns; it never finished a single song. A moment of silence — which is
  also what it always produced.
- Graphics errors now say what happened ("the driver stopped responding",
  "the card ran out of memory") instead of shrugging in hex.
- "Skip for now" in the setup wizard no longer silently kills a download in
  progress — it closes the window and lets the download finish in peace.

Mac:
- Nothing to re-download: Apple Silicon keeps its torch pack, Intel Macs
  their CPU pack — with a beat-detector model half the size, same beats.

Everywhere:
- The empty bass lane on very lo-fi recordings is the model being honest,
  not broken — some walls of sound simply have the bass welded in. We
  checked with two different AIs. And a bass specialist.
