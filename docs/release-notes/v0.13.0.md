v0.13.0 — smooth lyrics, honest sync

The phone stopped stuttering through your lyrics, and the cloud stopped quietly forgetting things.

## The lyrics glide now

On the phones, the karaoke line used to be built out of about seven stacked layers per word, rebuilt every time the line changed. On a 120 Hz screen it managed 58 frames a second — you could feel it.

The whole lyric column is now painted as one canvas, and the sung line is a single piece of text with a gradient sweeping through it (the same trick the desktop has always used). Same screen, same song: **121 frames a second, and 0.2% janky frames instead of 19.6%**. The sweep can now stop *inside* a letter, which is where singing actually happens.

Dragging through a long song got cheaper too — only the lines on screen are drawn, which took the busiest thread from 64% of a core to 44%. Taps and auto-scroll read the exact geometry the canvas draws, so tapping a line lands where you aimed.

## Your changes reach Drive on their own

This is the big one. **Four of the seven ways lyrics get written had no sync trigger at all** — a fresh fetch from LRCLIB, an AI transcription, picking a variant, a pending edit settling. They reached your phone only if some later save happened to sweep them along. And a sync requested while another was running was simply dropped on the floor.

Now every writer marks the library as changed and a scheduler owns the rest: it waits a few seconds for you to stop editing, never runs two at once, backs off when there's no signal, and picks up where it left off after a crash.

While that was being fixed, so was the trust problem: the sync now **asks Drive what it actually holds** rather than believing the notes it left itself last time. A file edited on Drive, deleted on Drive, or written by your other desktop is finally noticed, and leftovers from an old split stop haunting your phone's library. Still 4 requests for a library of any size.

Also in the department of not losing your songs:

- The ✓ badge now means *a sync that actually finished*. The old rule could tick a project a failed sync never touched, and never ticked a lyrics-only change at all.
- Stems upload before the project file, so a sync killed halfway leaves Drive *behind* rather than ahead — a phone can never meet a song that promises files Drive can't serve.
- Two new refusals: a library that reads as empty will not trash your Drive folder, and a cloud folder still downloading is no longer mistaken for an empty one.
- The Android Drive bug is fixed.

## The phone can finally tell you what happened

There's a Log in the phone app now, opened from the header — same view the desktop has, with Share where the desktop has Copy. A release APK has no debugger attached, so when something goes sideways on the couch, what the app wrote down is the whole story. (The desktop's sync record moved into that same one Log dialog, and what previous sessions wrote is replayed at launch — "the phone is showing yesterday's mix" is always a question about a session that already ended.)

## The melody line travels with the song

The pitch line is now saved into the project (~20 kB for a four-minute song) instead of being re-derived every single time you open it. Songs open quicker, and your **phones can draw the line at all** — they have no pitch tracker of their own. It's stamped with the version that drew it, so a future improvement quietly re-traces old songs rather than leaving them wrong forever.

## The count-in handles songs that change gear

Wild World counts eighths through its choruses and quarters through its verses — six changes of level in one song, same bars throughout. The old code asked the question once for the whole song and clicked the last third at half tempo. It now asks per section, and a beat that's already at your rate can belong to both sides.

## Small joys

- The library grid sizes itself to the window. Cards used to sit below the fold with no way to scroll to them.

**No new pack:** the splitter and beat model are unchanged, so there's nothing to download — update and carry on.
