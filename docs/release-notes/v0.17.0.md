v0.17.0 — the phone finishes the song it started

<!-- store:en-US -->
Split a song on your phone and it now finishes the job itself: the beat grid, the key and the melody are worked out on the phone, in about half a minute, so the click and the count-in follow the music on songs your phone made — no computer involved. Then it packs the six stems down from about 320 MB to 115 MB, losing nothing. A new Song sheet says what has been detected and lets you ask again. And a song whose artist tag carries a "feat." or a download-site name now finds its synced lyrics.

Android and iPhone:

- **A song you split on the phone is now a finished song.** Until this release
  the phone did the hard half and stopped: six stems, and nothing that knew
  anything about them. No beat grid, so the click had nothing to follow and
  the count-in counted seconds off the clock instead of the song's own bars.
  No key, and no melody line. It was a stack of stems you could sing over, and
  everything that makes it a SingZ song had to be worked out somewhere else.

  It does that itself now. When the split finishes, the phone reads the stems
  — the beat and the bar lines, the key, the sung melody — with the same
  detectors the desktop runs. On a mid-range Android phone it takes about half
  a minute, after the split you were already waiting on. Then the song is
  simply ready: the click follows the music, the count-in counts the song's own
  bars, and the Song sheet can tell you what key you are in.

  Songs you split under an older version get the same treatment — the phone
  notices what they are missing the next time you open the library and works
  through them.

- **Better beats, if you want them.** SingZ can also read the beat with the
  neural detector the desktop uses, which holds a grid through quiet intros
  and rubato that drums alone lose. Its models are an 87 MB download, once, so
  the phone offers rather than assumes: a card in the library with Download
  and Not now. Say no and everything above still works — you get the beat
  detector's own drums-first answer, without the neural second opinion. Songs
  that already have a grid keep it either way.

- **And then the song puts itself away properly.** Six stems of a five-minute
  song land as about 320 MB of raw audio, which is a real amount of a phone's
  storage. Once the analysis is done the song repacks itself into FLAC — 115
  MB, about forty seconds of work, and not one sample different. Nothing to
  press and nothing to choose: you will see "Compacting stems" go past at the
  end of the progress bar, and then have two hundred megabytes back per song.

- **A Song sheet, behind the ••• on the player.** Everything the app has
  worked out about the song, in one place: Beat — tempo, meter and bar count —
  then Key, Melody and Lyrics. Beat and Key each distinguish three things that
  used to look alike: known, being worked out right now, and genuinely not
  there. "No key in these stems", when the guitar, piano and bass lanes are
  silent, is an answer rather than a gap, and it is remembered rather than
  re-read every time you open the song. A song the phone split itself carries
  a Detect again under the Beat row, which re-reads all three.

Everywhere — phone, Mac and Windows:

- **The lyrics search stopped being fooled by how the tag spells the artist.**
  A song tagged `Женя Трофимов feat. Nansi & Sidorov` found nothing at LRCLIB,
  which has it under half a dozen credits, none of them containing "feat." —
  so on the desktop the app went off and spent three minutes transcribing a
  song somebody had already timed by hand, and on the phone it simply came up
  empty. Rips carrying a download site in the artist field
  ("AudioCleaner_Download_Notre Dame de Paris") missed the same way, one
  prefix short of a record sitting right there.

  Both are dropped and re-tried now — guests first, then the site name — but
  only after the tag as written has already come up empty, so nothing that
  finds lyrics today can start missing. The site name is spotted by its shape,
  not by a list of sites somebody has to keep up to date. On the desktop,
  songs already on disk that the old, blinder search told "no match" will
  quietly ask once more the next time you open them: a "no" is only as good as
  the question that produced it.

Mac and Windows:

- **Lyrics can no longer land in the wrong song.** Start a song, lose patience,
  open another one before the lyrics arrive, and the first song's lyrics could
  turn up in the second — measured, with Wild World cheerfully displaying
  "Metallica — Nothing Else Matters". Worse than it looks: the lyrics feed the
  beat detector's sense of phrasing, so the wrong words were baked into the
  right song's grid and saved there. Guarded now, and guarded by a test that
  loses the race on purpose.

- **A cancelled Precise alignment stopped shouting.** Pressing cancel wrote
  `[error] mms align failed (exit null)` into the log — the loudest line in
  the report, describing the thing you had just asked for. It says it was
  cancelled now.

Under the floor, and the reason this release is mostly numbers: every one of
those detectors moved out of JavaScript and into the C++ engine both phones
already share, one at a time, each checked against the desktop's answer before
the next one started — the melody agrees with it to the last bit, the key and
the beat grid agree as answers. That is where the half-minute comes from; the
melody alone went from about ninety seconds to about one.
