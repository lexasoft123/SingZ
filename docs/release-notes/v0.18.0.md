Mac and Windows — your songs are about to re-read themselves, and here is why:

- **The beat grid was listening to your speakers.** SingZ handed the beat
  detector the audio that was *playing*, which your computer had already
  resampled to whatever rate your output device runs at. The song's own file
  says 44.1 kHz; your Mac may well be playing it at 48. The detector was
  reading the second one.

  On a machine whose output happens to be 44.1 kHz the two are identical,
  which is how this survived so long. On a 48 kHz one, measured here with the
  neural model off: two of four test songs came out a whole octave off — one
  at exactly the tempo its own ground-truth entry records as a known wrong
  answer — and that answer was saved under a current stamp and synced to the
  phones, where nothing would ever re-derive it. Against the full ground truth
  the file rate scores 41 of 51 checks against the device rate's 40.

  Every detector now reads the stems from their files, at the rate each file
  states. **Your beat grids and your melody lines re-derive once**, on the next
  open of each song, and then stay put. Keys are untouched — measured across
  the whole test library the key comes out the same at either rate, so nothing
  re-reads them.

  Work you did by hand survives this, by two different routes. Anything you
  set yourself in the Metronome panel — a tapped or typed tempo, half or
  double time, a 10 ms nudge, a change of beats per bar, moving the "1" —
  marks that grid as yours, and nothing re-derives over it behind your back.
  Bar lines you merely nudged on a detected grid are the other case: they are
  stored as times and re-folded onto each new grid the app works out on its
  own, so that song gets the corrected detection *and* keeps your corrections.

  Both of those are about what happens by itself. The Re-detect button is a
  fresh start rather than a re-derivation, and today it does not carry
  hand-placed bar lines across — worth knowing before you press it on a song
  whose bars you fixed. That is older than this release and is being dealt
  with separately.

- **The melody was framed the same way.** If your computer plays at anything
  other than 44.1 kHz, the pitch line saved with each song was tracked from
  resampled audio: on one test song that is 8009 frames against the file's
  7998, with 4% of the voiced points disagreeing about whether a note was sung
  at all, and 5% of the frames they did agree on landing more than a
  quarter-tone apart — one of them by a whole octave. Worse, a phone reads the
  file and your computer read the speakers, and both stamped the result
  identically, so neither would ever have re-checked the other's answer.
  Every line re-tracks on the next open.

- **And an older copy of SingZ could quietly undo an update.** Every staleness
  check asked whether a saved analysis *differed* from what the app expects,
  when the question is whether it is *older*. So a machine still on the
  previous version, opening a library the new one had just improved, would
  re-derive the worse answer and save it back — one song per open, silently
  unwinding the whole thing. It upgrades and never walks backwards now — which
  means it is worth getting every machine onto 0.18.0, because a straggler
  still on 0.17.1 has the old rule and will keep undoing the new one.

- **One engine, not two.** The detectors that do all of the above are now the
  same C++ core the phones run, shipped beside the app. That is the whole
  reason the bugs above could be found at all: two implementations of the same
  detector disagreeing is a thing you can measure, and one implementation is a
  thing you cannot. A visible side effect: the pitch line appears seconds
  before the beat grid.

iPhone and Android — a pass over the whole app:

- **The hard bar goes round until it is right.** Mark where it starts, mark
  where it ends, and it loops. Tap once more to let it go. The band draws on
  the scrub rail so you can see what you caught. The engine has had this since
  the desktop needed it; nothing on the phone reached it until now.

- **The library got an order, a search box, and its actions out of the
  long-press** — or at least further out. For a song on the phone itself,
  swipe it left for Detect the beat again, Find lyrics and Delete; for a song
  from Drive, the swipe removes its downloaded copy. Delete and Find lyrics
  used to live *only* behind a long-press with nothing anywhere saying a
  long-press existed; asking for the beat again was not on a library card at
  all, only inside the player. A swipe is not self-announcing either, so this
  is an improvement rather than a cure. (Split has had its own button on the card
  since 0.17.)

- **The mixer answers the three questions you actually ask** — Full mix, No
  vocals, Vocals only — in one tap, on any song that has a vocal lane. The six
  faders are still underneath.

- **Every control can say its own name** to a screen reader, from a
  journey-by-journey pass over the whole app. The ones that blocked a journey
  outright are gone: the Add-a-song sheet sat under the keyboard — merely
  covered on iPhone, pushed clean off-screen on Android — so you could not see
  the title you were typing or reach a single button.

- **A new stage.** The player sits in a dusk-lit room, out of focus, with the
  chrome in smoked glass; the song shows in its stem tile and its seek bar
  rather than tinting the whole screen. The sheets scroll properly, a flick
  carries through instead of stalling, and the library shelf is still there
  when you come back instead of a spinner over it.

- **When something goes wrong it now says what.** One notice slot used to
  carry six unrelated failures, so whichever spoke last silenced the rest — and
  it ended "please report this" with nowhere to report it. Crashes get their
  own banner with a Report button that opens the Log, which has Share, which is
  how a phone log leaves a phone.

- Songs the phone split itself re-read their beat and melody once too, on the
  next open, for the same reason your computer's do. A song downloaded from
  Drive keeps whatever the desktop worked out for it.

Nothing here changes how a song sounds. It changes what SingZ believes about
it, and how much of the app you can actually reach.
