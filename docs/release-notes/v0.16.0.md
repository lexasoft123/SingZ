Phone — the big one:

- **Add a song without a computer.** Pick any track on your phone, and it's
  in your library: the app reads its length and tags, finds synced lyrics,
  and starts playing straight away as a single lane. If the lyrics come back
  wrong you can correct the title and artist and search again, or add it
  without them and find them later.
- **Split it on the phone.** Every unsplit song carries a Split button.
  Tap it and the phone separates the track into six stems by itself — the
  same model, and the stems match the desktop's to within a couple of bits.
  Expect it to take about as long as the song: a four-minute track is roughly
  four minutes on a decent Android phone, a little longer on an iPhone. The
  first split downloads the separator once, 136 MB, and never again. It needs
  a phone with 5 GB of memory or more — below that it says so up front and
  points you back at the computer rather than failing halfway.
- **It survives being interrupted.** Splitting keeps its place: kill the app
  mid-song and it offers to carry on from the chunk it reached, rather than
  starting the whole thing again. If a split genuinely gets stuck, it says
  so instead of spinning forever.
- **Keep the phone awake for now.** Splitting runs while the app is on
  screen. Leaving it pauses the work rather than losing it — background
  splitting is next.

Phone — smaller things you'd have noticed:

- A song sitting on your phone shows a tick, not a cloud. The cloud means
  "still on Drive, not here yet", and an added song was wearing it by
  mistake — on iPhone only, while Android showed the tick, for the same
  song.
- An unsplit song no longer claims "0 stems · 1 added" over music that is
  audibly playing. It says "not split yet", which is what it is.
- A song the phone has just split no longer tells you to go and update it
  on the computer.
- On a fresh iPhone, the first screen offers to add a song instead of
  advising you to save projects into a folder that mode doesn't use.
- The 136 MB separator download shows a progress bar that actually moves on
  iPhone. It always moved on Android.
- A file the phone cannot play now says so and offers a way out, instead of
  leaving two buttons that look alive and do nothing.

Windows:

- **If you haven't split anything since 0.15.0, your next split still fetches
  that 296 MB pack.** Nothing new here — it is the last release's engine
  change finishing the job, and it happens the first time you split, once.
  Already split something on 0.15.0? Then you have it, and there is nothing
  to do.

Desktop:

- Song keys are read from the harmony now, not from the sung melody. A song
  whose tune avoids its own root note used to come out wrong — Rammstein's
  "Zeit" reads G minor rather than A major. Existing songs re-derive their
  key the next time you open them.

Under the hood, for the curious:

- The phone's splitter is the same C++ engine on both platforms, and it
  matched the desktop on the first try.
- On iPhone, ONNX Runtime's model optimiser killed the app two seconds into
  every split — invisible on every simulator, on a phone with 3.3 GB free.
  Splitting now skips that step, which costs nothing in quality — the stems
  are unchanged. The app also keeps a record of its own memory and CPU that
  survives being killed, which is what found it. That record stays on the
  phone; it is there for the Log button, not for us.
