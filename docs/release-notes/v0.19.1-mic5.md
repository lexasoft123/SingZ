v0.19.1-mic5 — the microphone now goes through Google's own audio library

Chasing your phone turned up something bigger than your phone. SingZ talked to
the Android microphone directly, and Google publishes a library — Oboe — whose
main value is a list of phones that misbehave, kept up to date from reports
across every Android device there is. Reading it, one entry stood out: on some
Samsung Galaxy S9+ builds, the exact way SingZ asked for the microphone
records **silence**. Not an error. Silence. The request succeeds, the app looks
healthy, and nothing is ever heard.

That is precisely the fault you reported, sitting in wait on a different
phone — and SingZ would have shipped it, because the way it was written, a
request that succeeds is a request that worked.

So capture now goes through Oboe, and SingZ inherits that list instead of
rediscovering it one confused singer at a time. It also covers phones that
quietly record in stereo when asked for mono, two chipsets with broken
low-latency recording.

Measured on a test phone against a fixed tone, the old and new paths are
identical in level and in what they detect — this is not meant to make your
phone louder. It is meant to stop SingZ being the only app on the phone that
has to learn every device's quirks by hand.

**SingZ now needs Android 9 or newer.** Android 8 phones keep the copy they
have and it will go on working, but they will not receive this or any later
update. That is a deliberate choice. On Android 8.0 the new capture path cannot ask
for a specific microphone at all, which meant carrying a special case no phone
we have could ever test; 8.1 went with it because supporting one half of a
version nobody here runs is not worth the branch.

Everything from the last three builds stays: the level compensation, the
listening threshold set from your own log, and the one-channel request.
