v0.19.1-mic4 — SingZ asks the phone for one microphone, the way every other app does

Your observation is what turned this one round: a voice message recorded in a
messenger on that same phone sounds perfectly loud. So the microphone was
never weak, and the premise of the last two builds — "that phone is just very
quiet" — was wrong.

A phone reports its built-in microphone as having two channels, and SingZ,
built to work with recording interfaces where extra channels are extra inputs
you might want, asked for both and listened to the first one. That turns out
to rest on something Android does not promise. The platform documents how to
pick a channel by index, but nowhere says which physical microphone any index
is; the one API that does map microphones to channels is not available on the
low-latency capture path SingZ uses. And a phone's second microphone is
normally the one placed to hear the ROOM rather than you, so the phone can
subtract the room from your voice.

Measured here on a Xiaomi, the two channels differ by 4.4 dB on the same
sound. So which one an app listens to changes what it hears — but not even
that measurement can say which microphone is which, because the phone does not
tell anyone.

So SingZ now asks for a single channel, which is what the audio library's own
examples do for recording and what a messenger asks for. Nothing else changed.

What this does NOT do is prove your phone was listening to the wrong
microphone — that would need a measurement from your phone, and asking for one
channel removes the second one to compare against. What it does is stop
depending on an assumption the platform never guaranteed. If this was the
whole story, the log will show it: the "listening" line will read
`channel 1/1`, and the level beside `peak` should be far healthier than the
−43 to −50 dBFS it has been. If that line still says `channel 1/2`, the phone
declined the request and this build changed nothing for you — worth knowing
too.

One thing I would like either way: the `peak` figure from a session where
nobody sings at all. If this build makes your phone louder, its quiet room
gets louder with it, and that number is what says whether the level SingZ
treats as "someone is singing" is still set high enough.

The level compensation from mic-2 and the threshold from mic-3 both stay. They
were worth having regardless, and they are what keeps a genuinely quiet phone
working.
