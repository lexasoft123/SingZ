v0.21.1 — the computer stops jumping, starts on time, and streams like the phones

Nothing on your phone changed in this one — it is the Mac and Windows app catching up with what 0.21.0 gave the phone. Your phone already did all three.

Nothing here touches a song you already have: no grid re-derives, no melody
line re-tracks, no lyrics move. Three things the 0.21.0 desktop did badly,
fixed; the phones carry no change at all.

Mac and Windows:

- **Seeking stopped jumping to the start.** In 0.21.0, pressing Play or
  Pause, or clicking anywhere in the song, could send the seek bar to the
  beginning and back, and the Log filled with `seek failed · The absolute
  playback seek is invalid`. The desktop was telling the new engine that the
  song *began* wherever you last clicked, and then asking it to seek in
  whole-song time — so any seek into the last stretch of the song fell off
  the end of what the engine thought the song was and was refused, and the
  ones it accepted landed late. The phones never did this; the desktop now
  does what they do. Every seek lands, including the ones toward the end.

- **The seek bar keeps up with you.** A click on the bar took a fifth of a
  second to settle. The desktop was asking the engine "where are you now?"
  up to twenty-five times after every seek and waiting on the answers, when
  the actual seek in the file costs about a millisecond. It asks once now
  and shows your click at once; the bar catches up with the engine on the
  next status poll, which is what the phones have always done. Measured on
  a MacBook: a seek reads back in 11 to 26 ms where it took about 200.

- **Play starts the song in a moment.** Until now the computer decoded all
  six lanes to memory before every graph it built — about three seconds on
  a MacBook for a six-lane song, more on an older laptop — and it paid that
  again on any Play the graph it had prepared could not serve. The lanes now
  stream straight out of the song's files as it plays, the way they have on
  the phone since 0.21.0: the graph prepares in about twenty milliseconds
  instead of three seconds, and Play adopts it without a rebuild. The old
  Windows laptop SingZ is tested on prepared six lanes in 31 ms, played
  lighter than before (about 120 MB less while playing) and used less of a
  core doing it. Settings › Audio's native toggle still puts the old player
  back if you ever need it.

- **Lane waveforms stop going blocky when you zoom in.** The waveforms were
  measured at one fixed resolution for the whole song, so a close view of a
  few bars drew them as fat blocks. They are measured at the resolution the
  song's length needs now, and a zoomed view reads like a waveform again.

iPhone and Android: nothing changes here. This release carries no phone
change at all — the build you get under the new number is the 0.21.0 app,
rebuilt.

What actually happened: 0.21.0 brought the engine to all four platforms at
once, and the two phones had been living on it for weeks while the desktop
joined at the end. Two of the three fixes are the desktop being taught the
contract the phones already followed; the third is the desktop being given
the streaming the phones already had. The Windows half needed one more thing
on the way: on Windows two parts of the engine read the same song file at
once and could trip over each other, which the old Windows laptop SingZ is
tested on was the one machine to show. That is fixed too, and that laptop
is now part of every desktop check, not an afterthought.
