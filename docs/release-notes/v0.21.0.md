v0.21.0 — SingZ plays through its own engine now, and the phone stops decoding first

<!-- store:en-US -->
Songs open in a moment now. SingZ plays through its own audio engine on the phone: the lanes stream straight out of the song's files as it plays, instead of the whole song being decoded into memory first — so a long song no longer takes seconds to open, or most of your phone's memory to hold. The count-in lights each click as it lands. If you'd rather have the old player, Settings has the switch.

Nothing in this release touches a song you already have: no grid re-derives,
no melody line re-tracks, no lyrics move. What changed is the thing that
plays them.

Everywhere:

- **SingZ plays through its own engine.** Until now every SingZ handed the
  song to a browser's audio machinery — the Mac and Windows app to
  Chromium's, the phones to a wrapper around the same. The engine is SingZ's
  own now, written once and run on all four platforms: the six stems, pitch,
  tempo, the loop, the metronome, the count-in and vocal training all go
  through it. It is the default on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android, and the
  song should sound exactly as it did — that was the acceptance bar, replayed
  as a whole singer's evening on both engines and compared move for move.
  If something does sound different to you, Settings keeps the old player
  behind a switch — *Native playback* on the phone, *Use native DSP
  playback* under Settings › Audio on the computer — and a report with the
  song's name is very welcome.

  On an old Windows laptop — the slowest machine SingZ is tested on — Play
  now gets the song moving in about 0.18 s where it took 0.25–0.3, and a song
  that ran to the end restarts on Play in under a tenth of a second where it
  took a quarter. On a Mac the two engines measure the same; the gains there
  are elsewhere in this list.

Mac and Windows:

- **Opening a song shows a real progress bar.** The computer had a spinner
  for a wait that is two seconds on a MacBook and four on that old laptop;
  the phones have had a bar all along. Now the computer has one too, its
  steps sized from measuring where the time actually goes.

- **Right-click does something.** Until now a right-click anywhere in the
  window did nothing at all. In a text field it now offers Cut, Copy and
  Paste; on any selected text, Copy — the Log dialog being the place people
  wanted it most.

- **The lyrics credit is back.** Since 0.20 the row under the Lyrics header
  carries the source badge, the credit and three buttons in one line, and at
  the panel's width the buttons took every pixel: the credit — the LRCLIB
  attribution — was still there, at a width of zero. The row wraps now; the
  credit keeps its words and the buttons drop to a second line when they
  must.

- **Mac: about a quarter less CPU while a song plays.** Exactly one animation
  runs during playback — the pulse on the lyrics panel's long-pause
  countdown — and it was repainting a full-width glowing row sixty times a
  second. Windows froze it a while ago for the same reason, and stays
  frozen; the Mac never did. On the Mac it now steps sixteen times a second
  instead, which your eye reads as the same fade, and playing dropped from
  28% to 21% of a core on a MacBook. Not free, but the pulse is the seconds
  ticking down, so it stays.

Mac:

- **The microphone asks now.** Since 0.19.1 the Mac build has been signed and
  notarized — that is why it "just opens" — and a signed app runs under
  Apple's hardened runtime, where it has to declare that it wants the
  microphone. SingZ didn't declare it, so macOS refused without a word: no
  permission dialog, nothing to allow, no SingZ row under Privacy &
  Security › Microphone to switch on, and Match my singing and vocal
  training listening to silence. That's fixed — the first time you use the
  mic, macOS asks, the way it should have since 0.19.1. If you'd already
  gone looking in System Settings and found nothing there, this was why. And
  if it still won't hear you, the Log (the window's log dialog) now writes a
  `mic` line saying what the system answered, which is the thing to paste
  into a report.

- **Headphone monitoring, experimental.** Settings › Audio grows a section
  that plays your microphone back to you through your audio interface via
  the new engine, with the short latency that path allows. Pick the exact
  input device and its channel, and the interface's playback device; a
  meter and a live picture of the audio graph sit beside them. It stays on
  after you close Settings, with a small control on the main window to
  check on it or stop it. Mac only in this version — Windows lists its
  devices but keeps this off for now.

iPhone and Android:

- **Songs open in a moment.** The phone used to decode the whole song into
  memory before playing a note: a five-minute song, six lanes, is most of a
  gigabyte decoded, and took seconds to open. It also meant a long evening
  could end with the app quietly put down by the phone for using too much
  memory. The lanes now stream straight out of the song's FLAC files as it
  plays — nothing decoded ahead beyond a few seconds of read-ahead, nothing
  held — and the audio is the same bytes it always was. The seek bar's waveform is measured once in the
  background and kept with the song, so on a first open it fills in a lane
  at a time and on every open after that it is simply there.

- **The count-in lights one click at a time.** The dots along the count-in
  used to light together as one bar; each one now glows as its click lands,
  which is what a count-in is for.

- **Android: losing the audio to a call stops the song properly.** When a
  call or another app takes the audio, the song stops and the Log says why,
  and Play afterwards starts fresh.
