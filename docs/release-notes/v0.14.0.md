v0.14.0 — the metronome reads the score

Until now the beat grid was something the app *estimated*. As of tonight it is something the app can defend in an argument with the sheet music — we checked, against twenty-one published scores, and where they disagreed, the scores mostly lost politely.

## Songs land at the tempo the score says

Some songs make every beat tracker on earth hear double or half speed — the drums argue one thing, the singer feels another, and the notation quietly says a third. The detector now holds court over the octave: chord changes testify, quiet intros testify, the neural model's own conviction testifies, and two witnesses must agree before anything moves.

The verdicts, all score-verified: **Zeit** at 61 instead of 123. **Wish You Were Here** at 61.5 instead of 123. **Puppe** at 117 instead of 58.7. **Primo Victoria** and **Going To The Run** at 124.5 instead of 62.3. Ten other songs whose tempo was already right are protected by the same evidence — a court that can convict must also know when to acquit.

## The odd bars are found, not forgiven

That lone 2/4 bar before the chorus that always made the click limp for a moment? The 5/4 in Father and Son nobody warns you about? The grid now places them by itself — from chord changes, held notes and where the phrases breathe. One bar in the whole library still refuses to confess (a 3/4 hidden in a fingerpicked break); it ships wearing a red badge, one drag from correct, and we know its position to ten milliseconds. It knows what it did.

## Piano intros get real bars

A drumless intro used to be a no-man's-land the grid tiptoed around. Now the pulse is counted *backward* from the first steady stretch — the way a musician does — and re-locked on each chord actually played. Zeit's piano intro gets a bar line on every chord, at the score's tempo, from bar one.

## Solos stopped wobbling

Panzerkampf's guitar solo had every click *on a drum hit* — just not always the right one. The fills put accents between the beats and the tracker chased them, so the clicks lurched between perfectly placed bar lines. It now borrows the beat model's steady view of exactly that stretch and clicks straight through. 100% of the solo's beats on the pulse, up from 60%.

## The grid is yours to correct

Every bar line is draggable. Red badges mark the places the detector knows it was guessing — filled gaps, rebuilt intros, bars that disagree with the song's own meter — so you look exactly there first. Bars you place by hand are remembered as *times*, not positions, so they survive every future re-detection instead of being steamrolled by the next improvement. And the metronome window now says which detector version wrote the grid on screen, so "old grid" is a fact you can read, not a vibe.

## The melody reads like a score

The pitch strip now draws the vocal line as note bars — named pitches with real starts and ends, not a wiggly line. Glance at it the way you'd glance at sheet music, then match it with your voice. (Also fixed: it draws *this* song's melody, not the previous one's. Awkward.)

## Small mercies

- Saving a project can no longer silently delete an analysis the project already had. Grids stop vanishing at the exact moment you trusted them.
- The timeline stays where you scrolled it during playback; the page-turn glides instead of yanking.
- The beat grid rules the waveforms instead of shouting over them — lighter lines, calmer glow, lanes lit in their own colour.

## The bill

Every song re-tunes itself the first time you open it — give each one a few seconds while the new detector reads it. Hand-tuned grids are left exactly alone. **No new pack**: the splitter and beat model are unchanged, nothing to download.
