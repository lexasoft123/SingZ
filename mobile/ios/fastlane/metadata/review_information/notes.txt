SingZ is a rehearsal player for singers. A song is separated into its parts —
drums, bass, the other instruments and the vocals — rather than one recording,
so any part can be muted and sung or played live instead.

NOTHING IS NEEDED TO START REVIEWING

SingZ ships with one complete sample song — an original composition, not
licensed material — already in the library the moment the app opens. The six
faders, the synced lyrics, transposing, slowing down, looping, the lanes that
drop out while you sing (the Practice sheet) and the Train tab's exercises can
all be exercised on that sample alone, with no account, no download and no
second device.

BACKGROUND AUDIO (UIBackgroundModes: audio)

SingZ is a music player, and a song keeps playing when the singer leaves the
app or locks the phone — rehearsing usually means the phone is in a pocket or
on a music stand while you sing. To see it:

1. Open the sample song and press Play.
2. Press the side button to lock the phone, or swipe to the Home Screen. The
   song keeps playing.
3. The Lock Screen and Control Center show the song (title, elapsed time and a
   scrub bar) with play, pause and 10-second skip back and forward. Pause and
   play from there; drag the scrub bar and the song moves with it.

An earlier version (0.22.0) was rejected for exactly this, and it is fixed in
this build.
The sample song always plays through the older of the app's two audio engines:
it is unpacked from inside the app rather than read from files, so it cannot
use the newer one. Leaving the app silenced that engine whatever it was doing,
so the sample stopped the moment you pressed Home — as did any other song
playing on it, which includes any song at all with "Native playback" switched
off in Settings, or in a format the newer engine cannot open. Songs on the
newer engine kept playing, which is how this survived our own testing: we
tested with songs we had added ourselves, and the notes above sent you to the
one song it broke on.

All three steps above now behave the same for the sample as for any other
song: it keeps playing behind the Home Screen, and the Lock Screen's play,
pause and scrub bar reach it while the app is in the background. We checked
this from a fresh install on both an iPad and an iPhone (iOS 26 simulators),
and it is now an automated test that performs exactly those steps, which we
run whenever anything touches backgrounding or the audio engines.

WHERE ELSE SONGS COME FROM

Two further routes, neither required for review:

1. On the phone. "Add a song" opens the system file picker; the chosen audio is
   separated into six tracks on the device itself. Nothing is uploaded — there
   is no SingZ server. The first such song downloads the separation model
   (htdemucs_6s, ~136 MB) from the app's public GitHub release; this is model
   weights, i.e. data, not executable code. Separation is memory-hungry, so on
   a device with too little RAM the app declines with an explanation rather
   than attempting it.

2. From a computer. The free, open-source SingZ desktop app for Mac and Windows
   can prepare songs, and the phone reads them over Google Drive or from a
   copied folder.

NO ACCOUNT IS REQUIRED

There is no SingZ account and nothing to sign in to. Google Drive sign-in is
optional and only adds a second source of songs; it requests access solely to
files the app itself created (drive.file scope), never the rest of a user's
Drive. No feature is behind it.

MICROPHONE

The Train tab's exercises listen to the microphone to show whether a sung
note is on pitch. Audio is analysed live and never recorded,
never stored, and never leaves the device. iOS will ask for permission the
first time a listening feature is opened.

DATA COLLECTION

None. There is no SingZ server, no analytics, and no tracking. Songs stay on
the device.
